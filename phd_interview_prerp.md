# Questions Prep
- **pattern oriented validation** (to look for in Mayotte): frequency and intensity of local stakeholder non-compliance (threat appraisals override water restrictions) / successful adaptation behaviors through social influence and local proximity (replicate probability distribution of water-saving habits that spread through social networks in catchments)
- **staged coupling**: Hassan et al - Study of Tormes catchment (aggregate indicators show negligible change, intepreted as system resilience) / Dyanmic feedback can capture localized adaptations so that ML proxy can represent them at the basin scale. 
- **ML proxy**: emulated processes of ABM and hydrological model (using limited runs of full models for training data) through an artificial neural network. Relies on initial loose coupling (one way data flow), followed by calibraiton and subsequent two way feedback (model can simulate hydrologicla response to agents) / could implement physical knowledge (water balance equations or meteorological data) into the neural network architecture. 
- **synthetic population**: using iterative proportional fitting (algorithm that reweights survey data against census margins) can ensure that agents in the model are statistically representative of mayotte's actual population.
- **sub-grid HRUs**: dynamically sized hydrological response units, individual are represented on plot within a larger 1km grid, allows for household scale feedback without an impossible resolution.
- **Pattern oriented validation**: ensuring model reproduces multiple independent real world patterns (spatial clustering, temporal lags, behavioral variance) to calibrate it (the right results for the right reason).
- **surrogate modelling**: training ML proxies using data from full models (limited runs) to resolve hyper-resolution computational requirements enable larger scale modelling.
